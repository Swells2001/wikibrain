# Small-Group Hockey Skills Training (Boise)

Source key: [Ikigai 2] = Ikigai Part 2 worksheet. [Robin] = phone interview with a hockey parent, October 28, 2026. [Media Kit] = 10.05 Passion Project Media Kit. Anything else is marked NEEDS EVIDENCE.

## Problem

- Team practices focus on the whole team, so players may not get individual attention. [Robin: "Team practices focus on everyone, so sometimes kids don't get the individual attention they need."]
- Extra training splits into expensive in-person options and cheap online options with no feedback. [Ikigai 2 gap analysis]
- Private lessons cost about $50-$80 per session, by one parent's estimate. [Robin] She said it is "hard to justify paying that much for only 30 or 60 minutes."
- Camps cost $275 per player for three days and run only a few weekends a year. [Ikigai 2: Mylymok camps at Idaho IceWorld]
- Online courses cost about $178 (discounted from $249) and have no live coaching. [Ikigai 2: Online Hockey Training]
- Ice hockey averages $2,583 per child per year, the most expensive of 21 youth sports surveyed. [Aspen Institute Project Play, national figure]

## Who it is for

- Boise hockey players ages 8-12 and their parents. [Ikigai 2]
- Parents who want extra skill development without paying for private lessons. [Ikigai 2]
- How many such families exist in Boise: NEEDS EVIDENCE.

## My solution

Features (used by the MoSCoW framework):

1. Small groups of 4-6 players per session. [Ikigai 2, Robin agreed the size is "a good balance"]
2. Coaches chosen for experience teaching younger players, not only playing experience. [Robin: "Being a good hockey player doesn't automatically mean you're a good coach."]
3. Target price of $25-$35 per player per session. [Robin said she would be comfortable with this range]
4. A package or monthly option that saves a little money. [Robin]
5. A short written progress update each month: what the player does well, what to improve, things to practice at home. [Robin]
6. Sessions close and convenient. [Robin: would not drive 45 minutes for a one-hour session]
7. Reviews and recommendations from early families. [Robin]
8. Off-peak ice or synthetic ice for stickhandling, shooting, and puck control. [Ikigai 2 rebuttal; untested idea]
9. AI-drafted progress reports and session plans, reviewed by a coach. [Media Kit pitch work; planned, not built]
