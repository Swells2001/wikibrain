# Ikigai Project and Coursework

How [[Youth Hockey Skills Training]] (see [[My Project]]) connects to what Solomon is
learning in ITM 370 and International Econ. The ITM 370 links are direct: the project
used methods from that class. The econ links are analogies, not the same mechanism.

## ITM 370 — methods the project actually used
- **Dueling models became the AI stress test.** [[Noah]]'s idea of running work past an
  AI built to push back ([[Expert in the Loop]]) is what Part 2 did with Claude
  ([[AI Stress Test]]).
- **Expert in the loop, not just human in the loop.** Solomon didn't accept every AI
  point. He rejected the competition worry and kept the ice and profit worries. Round 2
  sharpened the differentiators to price, a trusted coach, and progress updates.
- **A real person outranked the AI.** [[Robin]]'s "a good player isn't automatically a
  good coach" ([[Customer Discovery]]) changed the plan more than the AI did.
- **Own ideas first, then AI.** The Part 1 passion and skill lists were written by hand
  before a chatbot suggested needs. This matches Noah's point about guarding creativity.
- **Greenfield effect = the open questions.** Ice cost, unit economics, coach vetting,
  and a pilot are the foundation to settle before scaling (my reading; the notes don't
  say this).
- **Curiosity beats resistance.** AI/programming is a skill-to-improve, and sports data
  analysis was one of the three finalists.

## International Econ — the same supply-and-price logic (analogy)
- **Scarce ice works like a quota.** An [[Import Quota]] caps supply and pushes the
  domestic price up. Boise has two rinks, so ice time is capped, sessions get crowded,
  and private lessons run $50–$80 ([[Market Gap Analysis]]).
- **The exclusive rink deal works like a license that earns rent.** Under a quota,
  whoever holds the license captures the [[Quota Rent]]. [[Idaho IceWorld]]'s exclusive
  City of Boise partnership is a similar scarce right, which is why ice access is the
  top risk.
- **Synthetic ice routes around the cap.** Off-peak or synthetic ice
  ([[Next Level Hockey Idaho]]) supplies the service without the constrained input.
- **A new entrant is a price taker.** Like a [[Small Open Economy]] taking the world
  price, the project prices inside a band the market already set: $25–$35 against
  $50–$80 private lessons and about $178 for online video.

The analogy has limits. A quota is a legal cap on imports. The rink problem is a local
shortage of facilities.
