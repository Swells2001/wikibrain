# ITM 370 — Week 9 notes

Pasted class notes: a reflection on [[Noah]]'s talk about working effectively with AI.

## Summary — Reflection on Noah's talk
- **Be an expert in the loop, not just a human in the loop.** AI writes what you ask
  for in the moment but doesn't reason about where the project is heading. Piling
  features on a weak foundation eventually breaks — Noah calls this the "greenfield
  effect." Takeaway: plan more, learn the basics, and don't accept answers you don't
  understand.
- **Curiosity beats resistance.** People who avoid AI entirely are most likely to be
  caught off guard; some employers won't hire people who refuse to use it. You don't
  have to love it, but you should know what it can do — keep trying new tools.
- **Don't let AI dull your creativity.** Using Suno felt shallow until the author
  supplied their own lyrics and let AI set them to music. Because AI makes decent
  results easy, extra personal effort is what makes the output feel like your own —
  start with your own ideas, then use AI to build on them.

### Question 3 — a practical use case buildable in a day
A "dueling models" setup: two AI models given opposite personalities (one critical,
one positive), running ideas/writing/research through both to see where they disagree.
It counters AI's tendency to just affirm your idea, since one model is built to push
back. Expect some errors while setting it up (normal). The result is a reusable
"second opinion" tool to run before submitting an assignment or starting a project.
