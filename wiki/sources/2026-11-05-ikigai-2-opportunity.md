# Ikigai Part 2 — Find Your Opportunity

By [[Solomon Wells]]. Niche: small-group youth hockey skills training in Boise, Idaho,
for players ages 8–12. Validates the [[Youth Hockey Skills Training]] opportunity
through competitor research, a customer interview, and an [[AI Stress Test]].

## 1. Competitor research
Narrowed to extra skills coaching for Boise players 8–12. Five competitors:
- **[[Idaho IceWorld]] / Boise Skills Academy** — Learn to Play + year-round clinics,
  camps, scrimmages; exclusive City of Boise partnership. Audience: most Boise youth,
  ages 4–17.
- **Mylymok camps (at Idaho IceWorld)** — 3-day skill camps, $275/player, pro/NCAA
  coaches (2026: Jul 17–19, Aug 21–23).
- **[[Next Level Hockey Idaho]]** — 2,000 sq ft indoor synthetic-ice space in Garden
  City; individual, semi-private, goalie, shooter, beginner, elite.
- **IdaHockey LLC** — small real-ice goalie pad; private/small-group goalie training,
  video review, mentorship.
- **Online Hockey Training** — self-paced video, ~$178 (from $249), lifetime access.

Market context: Aspen Institute Project Play found hockey the most expensive youth
sport (>$2,500/year per player); Boise hockey is booming despite few rinks.

## 2. Gap analysis
See [[Market Gap Analysis]]. The market splits into **expensive in-person** (private
lessons, camps) and **cheap online with no feedback**. The open middle: regular,
affordable small-group coaching (4–6 kids), a trusted coach, and a clear monthly
progress update to parents, not tied to scarce rink ice. The opening is that no program
found combines small groups + low price + regular parent communication.

## 3. Customer conversation
See [[Customer Discovery]]. Interviewed **[[Robin]]** (parent of a 10-year-old
competitive player) by phone on 2026-10-28.
- Team practices can't give individual attention; private lessons run $50–$80/session,
  camps a few hundred/week — hard to justify.
- A group of 4–6 kids feels like the right balance (compete + learn, coach has time).
- Would "definitely consider" at ~$25–$35/session; prefers a monthly package; wants a
  simple progress update (doing well / improve / practice-at-home).
- Blockers: price, scheduling, trust. "Being a good hockey player doesn't automatically
  mean you're a good coach." Wouldn't drive 45 min for one hour. Reviews from other
  parents matter.

**How it changed the idea:** prioritize coaches experienced at teaching kids (not just
playing); keep $25–$35 with a monthly package; build in monthly written parent updates;
keep sessions close; collect early reviews.

## 4. AI stress test
See [[AI Stress Test]]. Used Claude as devil's advocate.
- Challenges: ice access is scarce (two rinks; IceWorld–BSA exclusivity); the unit
  economics are tight ($100–$210/session must cover space, pay, insurance, fees);
  possible unneeded middleman; trust/vetting not yet established; thin evidence (one
  friendly parent); competitors could copy.
- Rebuttal: biggest worries are affordable ice and profitability; competition concern
  overstated; can try off-peak or synthetic ice, and ask Jackson Howell at Next Level
  Hockey about costs; differentiator = small groups + affordable price + parent
  communication.
- Round 2: off-peak/synthetic ice are smart testable savers; "personal experience" is
  too vague — real differentiators are Robin's three asks (price, trusted coach,
  progress updates); edge is being the trusted individual-attention option. Break-even
  (illustrative): $120/session cost -> 4 kids @ $30 breaks even, 6 kids @ $30 leaves $60.

## 5. Decision
Build small-group (4–6 kids) hockey skills training with monthly parent progress
reports for Boise players ages 8–12, because parents like Robin find private lessons too
expensive and camps too crowded. Chosen over athlete contract support and sports
performance/recruiting analysis as the most buildable and testable now.

**Still to find out:** ice/synthetic-ice costs and whether paid sessions can run at
Idaho IceWorld; whether $25–$35 covers costs at 4–6 kids; coach vetting (teaching
experience, background checks, certification, insurance); whether parents beyond Robin
will pay (pilot).
