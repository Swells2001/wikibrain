# International Econ — import quotas

Pasted class notes from International Economics covering import quotas, how they differ
from tariffs, and their effect in a small open economy.

## Summary
- A **quota** is a legal limit on the quantity of a good that can be imported.
- **Implementations:** absolute quota (imports can't exceed M units/year),
  tariff-rate quota / TRQ (low tariff up to a threshold, higher after), and licensing
  (firms need import licenses).
- **Quota vs tariff:** a [[Tariff]] generates government revenue; a quota generates
  [[Quota Rent]] (profit from being allowed to import under the cap).
- **Policy reasons:** protect domestic industry, stabilize domestic markets against
  import surges, shield sensitive sectors (agriculture, steel, textiles).
- **Small open economy (importer):** the country is small relative to the world
  market and takes the world price Pw as given. Under free trade Pd = Pw; at Pw firms
  supply Q1 and consumers demand Q2, and since Q2 > Q1 the country imports M = Q2 − Q1.
- **Under a quota:** the government caps imports at a fixed M. With imports limited the
  domestic price rises to Pq until the market clears — domestic supply rises, domestic
  demand falls, and imports stay fixed at the cap M.
- **Who captures the quota rent** depends on license allocation: if licenses are
  auctioned, the government collects the revenue.
