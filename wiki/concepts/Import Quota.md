# Import Quota

## Core
An import quota is a legal limit on the quantity of a good that can be imported. By
capping supply from abroad it raises the domestic price above the world price and
protects domestic producers, differing from a [[Tariff]] in how the gains are
distributed.

## Key Points
- **Absolute quota:** imports cannot exceed M units per year.
- **Tariff-rate quota (TRQ):** a low tariff up to a threshold, a higher tariff beyond.
- **Licensing:** firms need import licenses to bring goods in.
- **Vs a tariff:** a tariff raises government revenue; a quota creates [[Quota Rent]].
- **Policy reasons:** protect domestic industry, stabilize markets against import
  surges, shield sensitive sectors (agriculture, steel, textiles).

## Details
In a [[Small Open Economy]] that takes the world price Pw as given, free trade gives
Pd = Pw with imports M = Q2 − Q1. Imposing a quota that caps imports at M forces the
domestic price up to Pq until the market clears: domestic supply rises, domestic demand
falls, and imports stay fixed at the cap. Who captures the resulting [[Quota Rent]]
depends on how licenses are allocated — auctioning licenses sends the revenue to the
government.
