International Econ Notes

A quota is a legal limit on the quantity of a good that can be imported
Typical Implementations
Absolute quota: imports cannot exceed M units per year
Tariff-rate quota(TRQ): low tariff up to a threshold, higher tariff after
Licensing: Firms need import licenses to bring goods in
Key difference vs tariffs
A tariff creates government revenue. A quota creates quota rents( profits from being allowed
Economic/ policy reasons
Protect domestic industry: limit foreign competition
Stabilize domestic markets: cap import surges
Sensitive sectors: agriculture, steel, textiles( often)
Small open economy(importer)
Economic environment
The country is small relative to the world market
It takes the world price Pw as given
Under free trade: Pd=Pw
Imports under free trade
Under free trade, the domestic price equals the world price: Pd=Pw
At Pw, firms supply Q1 and consumers demand Q2
Since Q2>Q1, the country imports the difference
M=Q2-Q1

Market clearing under a quota
The domestic price rises to Pq such that:
Domestic supply rises(higher price encourages production)
Domestic demand falls( higher price discourages consumption)
Imports are fixed at M(by law)
The government sets an import quota that caps imports at a fixed amount M
With imports limited, the domestic price must rise until the market clears
